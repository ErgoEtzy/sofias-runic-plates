# Find contexts

## In the book

The results compare plates from graves with plates from residential settings such as towns, forts, workshops, and rural sites. The pattern is suggestive, but damaged texts and old assumptions can easily distort it.

[Book p. 309](../Sofia_Pereswetoff_Morath_Viking_Age_Runic_Plates_Readings_and_Interpretations.pdf#page=311) / [book note](<../Sofia Pereswetoff-Morath - Viking-Age Runic Plates.md>)

## Wider context

In archaeology, context means the place, layer, structure, and associated finds surrounding an object. A plate from a secure grave or floor layer carries more contextual information than a stray or detector find, even when the object itself is well preserved.
