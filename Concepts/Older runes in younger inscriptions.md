# Older runes in younger inscriptions

## In the book

Several plates have been read as containing forms from the older twenty-four-character futhark inside Viking-Age inscriptions. The book reviews these proposals cautiously because an apparent older rune can also result from damage, graphic variation, or an uncertain reading.

[Book p. 46](../Sofia_Pereswetoff_Morath_Viking_Age_Runic_Plates_Readings_and_Interpretations.pdf#page=48) / [book note](<../Sofia Pereswetoff-Morath - Viking-Age Runic Plates.md>)

## Wider context

The Elder Futhark and Younger Futhark differ in both inventory and sound values. A genuinely older form in a younger inscription may be an archaism, a learned choice, or a special sign, but the surrounding inscription has to support that conclusion.
