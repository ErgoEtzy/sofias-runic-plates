# Runiforms and rune-like signs

## In the book

A runiform looks like a rune but cannot be read securely as one. The term stays neutral when it is unclear whether a mark is improvised writing, a code, or simply a non-writing sign.

[Book p. 65](../Sofia_Pereswetoff_Morath_Viking_Age_Runic_Plates_Readings_and_Interpretations.pdf#page=67) / [book note](<../Sofia Pereswetoff-Morath - Viking-Age Runic Plates.md>)

## Wider context

The boundary is practical rather than absolute. A sign can move from 'unreadable' to 'readable' when a parallel inscription, better image, or new examination reveals a consistent system.
